When MIPI Byte Streaming Interface is enabled, a byte buffer (dual clock FIFO) is used to hold packets when 
downstream is not ready (axis_byte_tready_o is low). FIFO depth is configurable through the Byte Buffer Depth 
attribute. For multiple back-to-back packet, a full buffer leads to backpressure as shown in Figure 2.22. Byte buffer

the packet header, payload, and CRC bytes. Therefore, the maximum supported payload size is equal to the configured 
packet size minus 8 bytes.

2.4.2.4. MIPI CSI/DSI Receiver MBSI 
On the receiver side, when MIPI Byte Streaming Interface is enabled, the IP converts all MIPI short and long packets 
into AXI4‑Stream in the byte domain and handles backpressure. This conversion includes every packet type such as 
NULL and BLANKING packets whenever the packets are present.  
The IP maps the internal MIPI packet information (header, payload bytes, footer) to the corresponding AXI4 ‑Stream 
signals: TVALID, TREADY, TDATA, TUSER, and TLAST. Data movement strictly follows AXI4-Stream handshaking rules, 
where TVALID must be asserted by the source only when valid data is present, and TREADY must be asserted by the 
sink when the sink is ready to accept data. Transfer occurs only when both signals are high.  
You must implement any handling specific to packet type, such as identifying long or short packets or determining the 
footer byte position on a 32 or 64-bit TDATA bus. 
With MBSI in passthrough mode, converter IPs such as the Byte-to-Pixel IP are bypassed.
