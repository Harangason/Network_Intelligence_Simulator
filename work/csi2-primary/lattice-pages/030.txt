2.7.1.1. Initial Skew Calibration Operation 
When Initial skew calibration is enabled through the GUI, the skew calibration FSM in the TX controller issues a skew 
calibration pulse to the PHY after PHY initialization is complete. The PHY then transmits a calibration pattern across all 
active data lanes. When the calibration pattern transmission is complete, the TREADY signal on the IP interface is 
asserted, indicating that the IP is ready to accept data for HS transmission . Figure 2.28 shows the signal transition 
during initial skew calibration. 
Alternatively, initial skew calibration can be configured at runtime through the CSR register at address 0x220. The 
CAL_INITIAL_EN field controls whether initial calibration is enabled, and the T_SKEW_INITIAL field specifies the 
calibration duration in byte clock cycles. The IP also supports re-triggering the initial calibration process via the 
CAL_INITIAL_TRIGGER_EN field in the same register. This enables recalibration for systems where external devices have 
varying power-up times or where link conditions may have changed since the initial calibration. Suspend any incoming 
packets of the interval when initial skew calibration is re-triggered.

2.7.1.2. Periodic Skew Calibration Operation 
When Periodic skew calibration is enabled, the TX controller schedules calibration between image frames to avoid 
disrupting active data transmission. After a Frame End is transmitted, the TX controller waits for the PHY to enter the 
Stop state, indicating that the link is idle and ready for calibration. The scheduler in the TX controller then issues a  skew 
calibration pulse to the PHY, triggering a periodic calibration pattern across the serial links.  Figure 2.29 shows the signal 
transition during periodic skew calibration. 
Periodic skew calibration can also be enabled or disabled at runtime through the CSR register at address 0x220 via the 
CAL_PERIODIC_EN field. The calibration duration is configured through the T_SKEW_PERIODIC field.
