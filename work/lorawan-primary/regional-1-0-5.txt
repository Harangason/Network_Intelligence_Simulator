1 Conventions 357 
The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", 358 
"SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in 359 
this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and 360 
only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here. 361 
 362 
The tables in this document are normative. The figures and notes in this document are 363 
informative. 364 
 365 
Referenced document titles are written as LoRaWAN MAC Layer Protocol Specification and 366 
referenced section titles within this document are written as “Physical Layer”. 367 
 368 
The protocol commands discussed in this document are called MAC commands, they are 369 
written NewChannelReq, Bits, bit fields , and session attributes  are written CFListType, 370 
constants are written RECEIVE_DELAY1, variables are written N. 371 
In this document:  372 
• The octet order for all multi-octet fields SHALL be little endian.  373 
• By default, RFU bits are Reserved for Future Use and SHALL be set to 0 by the 374 
transmitter of the frame and SHALL be silently discarded by the receiver. 375 
• A range of integer values is noted as min_value-max_value, [min_value:max_value], or 376 
[min_value to max_value] where minimum and maximum values are included. 377 
• Random numbers SHALL be the output of either a true random generator, or a pseudo-378 
random number generator that is seeded differently for each end -device and on each 379 
re-initialization. Random(A-B) is a uniformly distributed random variable within range A-380

2 Introduction 385 
 386 
This document describes the LoRaWAN regional parameters for different regulatory regions 387 
worldwide. This document is a companion document to the various versions of the LoRaWAN 388 
MAC Layer Protocol Specification  [TS001]. Separating the regional parameters from the 389 
protocol specification allows addition of new regions to the former document without impacting 390 
the latter document. 391 
 392 
NOTICE:   LoRa Alliance provides all information herein in its capacity 393 
as a stakeholder in the promotion and adoption of secure, carrier-grade 394 
IoT LPWAN connectivity , and in the context of LoRa Alliance’s 395 
LoRaWAN standard.  This information does not constitute technical or 396 
legal advice and should not be relied upon to determine regulatory 397 
compliance in any given jurisdiction.   Each user of the information is 398 
responsible for its use of the information , including the need to  verify 399 
current information and its applicability to the user. 400 
 401 
 402 
The goal of the LoRa Alliance Regional Parameters Working Group is to allow the adoption of 403 
the LoRaWAN technology across as many regions of the world as possible. It is to the benefit 404 
of the entire eco -system to minimize the number of distinct channel plan definitions. By re -405 
using existing channel plans for regulatory regions  that are newly adopting LoRaWAN, the 406 
overall effort and the time required for products to be available in the new market is minimized. 407 
The Regional Parameters Working Group welcomes and encourages collaboration with local 408 
companies and regulators when LoRaWAN is considered for introduction to a new region.  409 
 410 
This document defines regional parameters described in all LoRaWAN Link Layer 411 
Specifications published prior to the publication of this document.  Where differences exist 412 
between LoRaWAN Link Layer specification revisions, they are highlighted in the text. 413 
 414 
Where various attributes of a transmission signal are stated with regard to a region or 415 
regulatory environment, this document is not intended to be an authoritative source of regional 416 
governmental requirements or regulations and we refer the reader to the specific laws and 417 
regulations of the country or region in which they desire to operate  in order  to obtain 418 
authoritative information. 419 
 420 
It must be noted here that, regardless of the parameters specified or configured, at no time is 421 
any LoRaWAN equipment allowed to operate in a manner contrary to the prevailing local rules 422 
and regulations where it is operat ing. It is the responsibility of the LoRaWAN end -device or 423 
gateway to ensure that compliant operation is maintained without any outside assistance from 424 
a LoRaWAN network or any other external mechanism. 425
