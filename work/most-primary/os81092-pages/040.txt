In a typical application, as illustrated in Figure 10-1, the power management circuitry is powered by a continuous supply
and manages the switched power to the rest of the node. This circuitry also monitors the activity signal coming from the
physical-layer interface (e.g. NOACTIVITY in Figure 10-1). When network activity is detected, the circuitry enables the
switched supply and drives the PS[1:0] pins to the proper state, as depicted in Figure 10-2. When INIC powers up, it
could take up  to tstp time for INIC to recognize the PS[1:0] state. When INIC is ready for shutdown, it releases the
PWROFF pin (pulled high through an external resistor).
The EHC can also implement a PWRHOLD line that the power management circuitry detects. This signal is used to sus-
pend node power-down until the EHC is ready. Refer to the MOST INIC Hardware Concepts Specification [8] for more
information on typical power management architectures.

Microchip’s Automotive Power Management Device  (MPM85000) performs all power management functionality
required of MOST Network nodes, including: wake detection, power supply monitoring (PS[1:0]), reset generation, and
optional Switch-To-Power (STP) pulse detection. The MPM85000 also supplies a 3.3 V continuous output which can be
used to power VDDU. Integrating both the OS81092 and MPM85000 on a MOST Network node minimizes application
circuitry and software complexity, allowing the application de signer to focus on the application, rather than on MOST
compliance. Contact Microchip for more information on the MPM85000.
