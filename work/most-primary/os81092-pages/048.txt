14.1 ePHY Front End Circuitry
In order for data transmission to be reliable over a wide operating rage, all signals along the transmission path need to
be relatively noise free, with minimal jitter and pulse width distortion. As a result, both the Transmitter and Receiver Front
End Circuits are designed for reducing electrical emissions, increasing system immunity to EMI, and minimizing reflec-
tions.
Figure 14-2 shows the recommended Transmitter and Receiver Front End Circuitry at the ePHY interface. Circuit com-
ponents used must comply with the guidelines provided in Section 16.8 “Physical Layer”. Use of an isolated chassis
ground on the cable side of the transformer (as shown in Figure 14-2) is optional.
FIGURE 14-2: ePHY TRANSMITTER/RECEIVER FRONT END CIRCUITRY
14.1.1 TRANSMITTER FRONT END
The Transmitter Front End Circuitry in Figure 14-2 includes the following components: C17, C18, C19, C21, C22, C24,
J1 (pins 2 and 3), L5, L6, L8, R2, R3, and T1 (bottom half).
The external load resistors on ETXP and ETXN (R2 and R3), in conjunction with the external load resistance reflected
back through the transformer, convert the current loops into a differential volt age that is approximately 1.2 V peak-to-
peak. Ferrite beads (L5 and L6), in conjunction with capacitors C17, C18, and C19, form a low-pass filter that removes
out-of-band frequencies caused by the digitally switched current loops. C17 and C19 also help reduce common-mode
noise. The transmission signal is passed through the 1:1.5 transformer (T1), with center tapped primary and secondary
windings. The primary center tap is connected to signal ground through capacitor C21; the secondary center tap is con-
nected to chassis ground through C22 and C24.
Transient voltage suppression devices (D202) are used for ESD protection on ETXP and ETXN. These devices are
avalanche breakdown diodes that offer protection against high voltage, fast-edge transients. If further ESD protection
is required to meet OEM specifications, an additional suppression device (not shown in Figure 14-2) may be placed on
the chip-side transformer center tap, in parallel with C21.
These circuits minimize the common-mode noise that may be picked up by the cable in a harsh electrical environment
and coupled into the system. Common-mode noise in both directions is also attenuated by a common-mode choke built
into the transmit portion of the transformer and by an additional common-mode choke (L8) on the cable side of the trans-
former.
