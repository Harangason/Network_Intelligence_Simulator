14.1.2 RECEIVER FRONT END
The Receiver Front End Circuitry in Figure 14-2 includes the following components: C15, C16, C20, C23, C25, C206,
J1 (pins 6 and 7), L2, L3, L4, L7, R4, R201, R202, and T1 (top half).
The differential receive signal on the cable (connected at  J1), is passed through a common-mode choke (L7) and is
applied to the 1:1 isolation transformer (T1). A choke intern al to the transformer (on the cable side) blocks incoming
common-mode noise. The center tap of the chip side of the transformer is connected to a capacitor (C20) which atten-
uates any residual common-mode noise passed through the transformer.
Transient voltage suppression devices (D201)  are also used for ESD protection on ERXP and ERXN. If further ESD
protection is required to meet OEM specifications, additional suppression devices (not shown in Figure 14-2) may be
placed on the chip-side transformer center tap (in parallel with C20) and on the ERXCM pin (in parallel with C206).
The output of the transformer is low-pass filtered by C15, L2, and L3 before reaching ERXP and ERXN. This low-pass
filtering causes the input impedance (looking into J1) to vary across the full frequency band of interest. As a result, shunt
components C16, L4, and R4 act as a tuning circuit, designed to keep the input impedance constant over the applicable
frequency range. This minimizes cable reflections and results in higher signal quality.
The receiver input is terminated with a pair of resistors R201 and R202, to match the cable impedance. These resistors
are connected directly between ERXCM and ERXN/ERXP. A DC voltage of approximately 1.2 V is generated by the
OS81092 at ERXCM, which sets the common-mode operating voltage of the received signal. This applied voltage
makes the received signal compatible with the requirements of the differential reception circuitry.
