L329@P5: Configuration Register
L330@P5: The DS2482-100 supports three 1-Wire features that are
L331@P5: enabled or selected through the Configuration Register.
L332@P5: These features are:
L333@P5: ● Active Pullup (APU)
L334@P5: ● Strong Pullup (SPU)
L335@P5: ● 1-Wire Speed (1WS)
L336@P5: These features can be selected in any combination. While
L337@P5: APU and 1WS maintain their state, SPU returns to its
L338@P5: inactive state as soon as the strong pullup has ended.
L339@P5: After a device reset (power-up cycle or initiated by the
L340@P5: Device Reset command), the Configuration Register
L341@P5: reads 00h. When writing to the Configuration Register, the
L342@P5: new data is accepted only if the upper nibble (bits 7 to 4)
L343@P5: is the one’s complement of the lower nibble (bits 3 to 0).
L344@P5: When read, the upper nibble is always 0h.
L345@P5: Active Pullup (APU)
L346@P5: The APU bit controls whether an active pullup (controlled
L347@P5: slew-rate transistor) or a passive pullup (RWPU resistor)
L348@P5: is used to drive a 1-Wire line from low to high. When
L349@P5: APU = 0, active pullup is disabled (resistor mode). Active
L350@P5: pullup should always be selected unless there is only a
L351@P5: single slave on the 1-Wire line. The active pullup does
L352@P5: not apply to the rising edge of a presence pulse or a
L353@P5: recovery after a short on the 1-Wire line.
L354@P5: The circuit that controls rising edges (Figure 2) operates as follows: At t1, the pulldown (from DS2482-100 or
L355@P5: 1-Wire slave) ends. From this point on, the 1-Wire bus is
L356@P5: pulled high through RWPU internal to the DS2482-100.
L357@P5: VCC and the capacitive load of the 1-Wire line determine
L358@P5: the slope. In case that active pullup is disabled (APU = 0),
L359@P5: the resistive pullup continues, as represented by the solid
L360@P5: line. With active pullup enabled (APU = 1), and when at t2
L361@P5: the voltage has reached a level between VIL1(MAX) and
L362@P5: VIH1(MIN), the DS2482-100 actively pulls the 1-Wire line
L363@P5: high, applying a controlled slew rate as represented by
L364@P5: the dashed line. The active pullup continues until tAPUOT
L365@P5: is expired at t3. From that time on the resistive pullup continues. See the Strong Pullup (SPU) section for a way to
L366@P5: keep the pullup transistor conducting beyond t3.

L751@P14: General Characteristics
L752@P14: The I2C bus uses a data line (SDA) plus a clock signal
L753@P14: (SCL) for communication. Both SDA and SCL are bidirectional lines, connected to a positive supply voltage
L754@P14: through a pullup resistor. When there is no communication, both lines are high. The output stages of devices
L755@P14: connected to the bus must have an open drain or open
L756@P14: collector to perform the wired-AND function. Data on the
L757@P14: I2C bus can be transferred at rates of up to 100kbps in
L758@P14: standard mode and up to 400kbps in fast mode. The
L759@P14: DS2482-100 works in both modes.
L760@P14: A device that sends data on the bus is defined as a
L761@P14: transmitter, and a device receiving data is defined as a
L762@P14: receiver. The device that controls the communication is
L763@P14: called a master. The devices that are controlled by the
L764@P14: master are slaves. To be individually accessed, each
L765@P14: device must have a slave address that does not conflict
L766@P14: with other devices on the bus.
L767@P14: Data transfers can be initiated only when the bus is not
L768@P14: busy. The master generates the serial clock (SCL), controls the bus access, generates the START and STOP
L769@P14: conditions, and determines the number of data bytes
L770@P14: transferred between START and STOP (Figure 7). Data
L771@P14: is transferred in bytes with the most significant bit being
L772@P14: transmitted first. After each byte follows an acknowledge
L773@P14: bit to allow synchronization between master and slave.
L774@P14: Slave Address
L775@P14: The slave address to which the DS2482-100 responds
L776@P14: is shown in Figure 8. The logic state at the address pins
L777@P14: AD0 and AD1 determines the value of the address bits A0
L778@P14: and A1. The address pins allow the device to respond to
L779@P14: one of four possible slave addresses. The slave address
L780@P14: is part of the slave address/control byte. The last bit of the
L781@P14: slave address/control byte (R/W) defines the data direction. When set to 0, subsequent data flows from master to
L782@P14: slave (write access); when set to 1, data flows from slave
L783@P14: to master (read access).

L824@P15: The following terminology is commonly used to describe
L825@P15: I2C data transfers. The timing references are defined in
L826@P15: Figure 9.
L827@P15: Bus Idle or Not Busy: Both SDA and SCL are inactive
L828@P15: and in their logic-high states.
L829@P15: START Condition: To initiate communication with a
L830@P15: slave, the master must generate a START condition.
L831@P15: A START condition is defined as a change in state of
L832@P15: SDA from high to low while SCL remains high.
L833@P15: STOP Condition: To end communication with a slave,
L834@P15: the master must generate a STOP condition. A STOP
L835@P15: condition is defined as a change in state of SDA from
L836@P15: low to high while SCL remains high.
L837@P15: Repeated START Condition: Repeated STARTs are
L838@P15: commonly used for read accesses to select a specific
L839@P15: data source or address to read from. The master can
L840@P15: use a repeated START condition at the end of a data
L841@P15: transfer to immediately initiate a new data transfer following the current one. A repeated START condition
L842@P15: is generated the same way as a normal START condition, but without leaving the bus idle after a STOP
L843@P15: condition.
L844@P15: Data Valid: With the exception of the START and
L845@P15: STOP condition, transitions of SDA can occur only
L846@P15: during the low state of SCL. The data on SDA must
L847@P15: remain valid and unchanged during the entire high
L848@P15: pulse of SCL plus the required setup and hold time
L849@P15: (tHD:DAT after the falling edge of SCL and tSU:DAT
L850@P15: before the rising edge of SCL; see Figure 9). There is
L851@P15: one clock pulse per bit of data. Data is shifted into the
L852@P15: receiving device during the rising edge of SCL.
L853@P15: When finished with writing, the master must release
L854@P15: the SDA line for a sufficient amount of setup time
L855@P15: (minimum tSU:DAT + tR in Figure 9) before the next
L856@P15: rising edge of SCL to start reading. The slave shifts out
L857@P15: each data bit on SDA at the falling edge of the previous SCL pulse and the data bit is valid at the rising
L858@P15: edge of the current SCL pulse. The master generates
L859@P15: all SCL clock pulses, including those needed to read
L860@P15: from a slave.
L861@P15: Acknowledge: Typically a receiving device, when
L862@P15: addressed, is obliged to generate an acknowledge
L863@P15: after the receipt of each byte. The master must generate a clock pulse that is associated with this acknowledge bit. A device that acknowledges must pull SDA
L864@P15: low during the acknowledge clock pulse in such a
L865@P15: way that SDA is stable low during the high period of
L866@P15: the acknowledge-related clock pulse plus the required
L867@P15: setup and hold time (tHD:DAT after the falling edge of
L868@P15: SCL and tSU:DAT before the rising edge of SCL).
